## Primary authorial position

Lawson-Tancred describes metaphysics as a rational search for a comprehensive, secure account of reality. He is strikingly sceptical about the literal success of historical metaphysical conclusions while arguing that the *arguments themselves* can remain philosophically magnificent and illuminating. He then proposes an account of the Aristotelian enterprise organized around **what exists (being), how things change, and whether they can be known (intelligibility)**. The `Categories` makes particular individuals basic; `Physics` explains substantial generation by matter and form, risking the priority of formless matter; `Metaphysics` tries to unite what is metaphysically fundamental with what is intelligible.

In his reading of Zeta, essence and definable species or **special substance** become central, subject to strong objections about unity of definition and the relationship between species and universals. In Eta, particular composites regain priority by a **potential-persistent** argument associated with **M. L. Gill**. Lawson-Tancred regards these reconstructions as ingenious and deeply important, but **does not claim that they are beyond criticism**; he openly questions their viability and declines to decide whether the difference between Zeta and Eta reflects an intellectual change of mind or a chosen order of teaching.

In §2, he treats the received fourteen-book text as a specialist, esoteric and unfinished work. He considers the alternative of extensive Lyceum compilation (associated with **Felix Grayeff**) but argues for a substantial plan that Aristotle may have designed, albeit without a completed literary finish. Kappa is a particularly awkward part of this reconstruction. In §3, he defines his translation as **introductory rather than definitive**, deliberately opening the text to readers early in their philosophical study.

These source-specific evaluations, objections and uncertainties are integral and are **not** to be replaced by the more categorical claims of the four separately supplied “Total Recall” guide drafts.

## Special fidelity and edition safeguards

1. **The source author must be visible.** The chapter frontmatter credits Hugh Lawson-Tancred, translator and introduction author. It must not say merely “Source: Metaphysics Companion” or name Aristotle as the author of the modern argument.
2. **Textual claims versus editorial interpretation:** the proposition that species constitute primary substances in Zeta, the precise distinction from universals, and Gill’s potential-persistent defence are reconstructions, not indisputable facts attributed directly to Aristotle.
3. **Sceptical value judgment:** the introduction’s criticisms of grand metaphysical arguments are part of the source and are not to be replaced by categorical praise of metaphysics as unquestionably successful.
4. **History is cautiously reconstructed:** the discussion of Aristotle’s father, Academy years and early idealism is plausible narrative as the author explicitly marks it, not documentary certainty.
5. **Do not conflate these neighbouring sources:** Dossier 2.2.5’s author is **W. D. Ross** (1924); 2.2.6’s author is **Hugh Lawson-Tancred** (1998). Ross focuses on the textual history, Lawson-Tancred on the substance project and interpretive architecture. Both credit Aristotle for the ancient work.
6. **The primary source is not uploaded or published.** Neither the full clipped PDF nor the supporting user-provided recall guides, language profile or excerpts become public GitHub assets.
