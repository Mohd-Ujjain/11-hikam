## Corrected discrepancies in the previous edition

1. Restored **Marx's methodological challenge and Bird's explicit refusal to endorse it before examining traditional approaches**. The older dossier began with Louis XIV and omitted the source's opening transition.
2. Credited **Colin Bird**, chapter 2 and original title. The old frontmatter used the generic “Cambridge Companion” label and no author.
3. Distinguished Aristotle's position from **the later Latin expression** `res publica`. Bird does not say Aristotle coined the Latin term.
4. Preserved Bird's description of positive morality as **likely an important part of a plausible answer**, not an exhaustive explanation of collective identity.
5. Kept `de jure` **rule-based authorization** distinct from `de facto` enforcement; did not infer that every formally authorised state is just.
6. Retained **both** forms of civic failure: unilateral slavery/domination and deep factional violence.
7. Restored the **two limitations** of the game analogy and the source's restricted use of Pacifica/Atlantis; no invented egalitarian/caste description.
8. Distinguished Plato's test of **actual benefit** from the minimum condition of **not harming** adherents; restored the need for a working conception of well-being.
9. Preserved Bird's strong presentation of Plato's challenge to liberal democratic morality, while retaining the text's **“If Plato is right”** condition. The original extract does not complete a case study demonstrating that conclusion.
10. Replaced the dependent revision Key Table pointer with an **independent** comparative table; preserved all six revision sections and five examination categories.
