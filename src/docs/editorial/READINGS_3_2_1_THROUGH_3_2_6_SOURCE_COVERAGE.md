## Editorial map

- **3.2.1:** pp. 165–168 presential and acquired knowledge, imperative of trust and four introductory questions; pp. 169–171 rival expectations and seven-step formulation; pp. 172–175 predisposition, recognition and return under duress; pp. 176–184 experiential and developmental evidence, reported explanations, purpose and protest; p. 185 film illustration and the beginning of Kant. Preserve the difference between acquired belief at birth and original disposition, and between research examples and the inference itself.
- **3.2.2:** pp. 221–227 formulation and psychological force; pp. 228–233 definition and arguments for objectivity; middle sections on nihilism, testimony concerning the ground of values, and claims and experiences ascribed to distinct speakers; dialogue on the gap between behavior and its ground; pp. 253–262 series of objections and limits of the available conclusion. The dossier distinguishes what Dawkins is reported as saying from what the author infers.
- **3.2.3:** pp. 441, 443–448 distinctions from broad design and providence, rivalry of readings, and argument; pp. 449–452 evidentiary plan, fragility and probability illustrations; pp. 452–457 law types, interplay of forces and constants; pp. 457–460 initial conditions and the distinct work of the cited examples; pp. 460–461 Henderson and Denton, with water, carbon and other constituent properties. Probability estimates are presented as part of the author's treatment, not an independent computation.
- **3.2.4:** pp. 95–98 names and two levels; pp. 99–104 scriptural reasoning and disputation; pp. 104–111 the two premises, axiomatic causality and regress of dependent causes; pp. 111–115 this cosmos versus the kind of created events, change in cosmological judgments, and Ibn Hazm's exclusion of alternatives; pp. 116–119 and final leaf expansion, background radiation, helium, Olbers and entropy. A scientific sign of beginning is not the causal principle or the end of the argument.
- **3.2.5:** pp. 14–15 definitions and the author's two cautions; pp. 15–19 movement, books and differentiation from Communist and older elite atheism; pp. 19–32 conditions of Arabic reception and three types of causes; pp. 33–35 advocacy and features of reasoning; pp. 91–97 spatial-dimension illustration and the three external considerations. The final argument about practical and social effects is explicitly distinguished from proof, and the reported observations are not converted into clinical instructions or population-wide findings.

- **3.2.6:** pp. 5–10 three announcements and the meaning of awareness; pp. 11–23 order, change and origination, with their different premises; pp. 24–38 habituation, psychological barriers, learning and proximate natural causes; pp. 39–56 rules of inquiry, Flew and the author’s own path; pp. 59–70 three answers, including why an obscure purpose does not itself constitute counterevidence.
