## Critical corrections to the prior 2.1.4 dossier

1. **Attribution:** “Oxford Companion” / generic subtitle have been replaced with **original author Jonathan Wolff**, the original book title, publisher and discontinuous printed pages. The single reader chapter title remains the established combination “Introduction & Who Should Rule?”.
2. **Original Introduction:** restored Locke's deliberately qualified formulation, the wealth/power connection, normative/descriptive interdependence, philosophical method, the limited individual's voice, and Wolff's express intention to leave the reader free to decide.
3. **Democracy's triad and history:** restored government <em>of</em> the constituency and the **distinction** between beneficial despotism and self-rule. Wolff's “term of praise” scepticism is no longer mispresented as his own categorical conclusion.
4. **Democratic plurality:** separated majority/minority rights, direct/representative governance, sortition, strength of minority preference, franchise and voting systems, without treating them all as one paradox.
5. **Plato's challenge:** restored the source's deliberate effort to make Plato's craft objection as strong as possible; details of ship and full training are accurately identified as Plato's, not independent recommendations.
6. **Critique of guardianship:** restored the distinction between advice and unrestricted power, why no-private-property is not self-enforcing, reluctant rulers and appointment/acceptance; importantly, Wolff says these objections **do not prove democracy**.
7. **Fallibility:** the former draft failed to make clear that Wolff **rejects** the general sceptical reply to expertise. Relative professional competence remains real; the stronger challenge concerns politically relevant **interests**.
8. **Road, Hume and polling:** citizens have competing views that philosophical expertise cannot alone disclose; Hume warns about short-term errors; surveys can reveal wants more sensitively than an election. This is not already a vindication of collective self-rule.
9. **Student smoking:** restored the original precise two-way motivation table; the voting result cannot be read as either the majority's desire or majority opinion about common good.
10. **Condorcet:** the source names **two necessary conditions**: average competence above even and disinterested common-good voting. The opposite threshold makes error overwhelmingly likely; personal motivations re-create ambiguity. Wolff does not present a freestanding certainty claim.
11. **Two valuations of democracy:** reclaimed both *instrumental* and *intrinsic* value, especially the original table-making and training-voyage counterexamples to Plato; intrinsic does **not** mean outcomes are wholly irrelevant.
12. **Source endpoint:** no completed Rousseau “general will solution,” lifelong civic programme or unconditional democratic vindication is imported from secondary working summaries. Wolff stops at the question and heading, with only the **beginning** of the next section's epigraph.
