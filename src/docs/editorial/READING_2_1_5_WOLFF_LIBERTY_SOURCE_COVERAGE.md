# Dossier 2.1.5 — Jonathan Wolff, *The Place of Liberty*

**Original source authority:** Jonathan Wolff, *An Introduction to Political Philosophy*, Oxford University Press, revised edition (2006), Chapter 4, **“The Place of Liberty,”** supplied original extract **printed pp. 104–117 (14 PDF pages)**. Source begins with Mill's liberty quotation and the heading “One simple principle.” Its final paragraph **stops immediately after** Wolff observes that one utilitarian law, “Act to maximize happiness,” is **“not so obvious”** a suitable legal system. No further argument or conclusion from later pages is included or supplied.

**Authorial ownership:** Wolff writes the assigned textbook chapter. Mill, Rousseau, Bentham and Locke are thinkers and authorities discussed by Wolff, not the author credited for the original reading. Quotations from Mill's *On Liberty* and *Utilitarianism*, Rousseau's *Discourse on the Arts and Sciences*, and Bentham's writings carry their own original page numbers within Wolff's quoted citations, which are distinct from Wolff's chapter pagination.

**Source discipline:** Three uploaded “Pasted text” master guides were consulted only as working maps, never as the primary authorial record. They sometimes give an apparently completed proof of indirect utilitarianism and convert the Alexandria account into a verified historical event; neither claim matches the **scope or evidential confidence** of the supplied primary PDF. Do not import such conclusions from those secondary study aids. No uploaded source PDF or supporting derivative study guides are committed to the public repository.

## Important corrections made to the previous dossier

1. **Original author credit:** Generic “Oxford Companion” and uncredited Mill description replaced with **Jonathan Wolff**, exact original textbook title, edition and printed pages; TOC source credit updated without changing navigation IDs.
2. **Freedom versus authority:** Democracy does not settle the legitimacy of coercion; both state force and social pressure remain open to scrutiny. Mill's anti-anarchist reasoning and anarchy/absolutism comparison kept intact.
3. **Harm Principle and its exceptions:** Preserved distinction between harm to others and paternalistic intervention, but the friend's addiction appears as **Wolff's hard question**, not a completed general legal verdict; Mill's own competence and societal exclusions remain attributed.
4. **No unsolicited moral verdict:** Deleted the old “Common Mistake” card's independent modern denunciation of Mill's historical exclusions and the unverified assertion about how examiners would mark such an answer. Source faithfully explains Mill's condition without endorsing it.
5. **Freedom-of-thought logic:** Kept the full true/false/partly-true structure, fallibility reasoning, and why genuinely true beliefs need challenge. Historical examples are source-author reports, not inserted external research.
6. **Alexandria historical qualifier:** The old and derivative master-guide treatments risked presenting the alleged library destruction as established. Wolff expressly says Abulfaragius is an apparently unreliable source; the visible dossier flags that fact and the optional source record retains the story strictly as a reported example.
7. **Rousseau and Wolff's residual objection:** Restored the objection that truths might damage social utility and the actual strong response by Mill; also restored Wolff's warning that the reply alone **does not settle the preference for free expression** without a general truth–happiness connection.
8. **Evolution/creation science caution:** Preserved Wolff's “according to some accounts” qualifier rather than turning the secondary explanatory case into a new firm empirical conclusion.
9. **Actual speech boundary:** The corn-dealer case makes clear that Mill's protection of discussion does not imply identical freedom for speech likely to cause immediate harm in a particular setting.
10. **Self/other-regarding test:** Restored private religious worship, offence, footwear and sleeping positions as a challenge to defining harm by *any* social effect.
11. **Competitive harm:** Interests may really be diminished when someone loses a competition, but that alone is **not sufficient** for justified interference; the source also raises whether injury to interests is always a **necessary** condition. Avoid premature equations of “harm” with already-proven rights violations.
12. **Rights and utility:** Preserved all intermediate questions: natural-rights scepticism, Bentham's legal creation claim, Locke's objection, the inability to rely on prevailing laws and Mill's proposed general-utility foundation.
13. **Exact stopping point:** The PDF stops with the *beginning*, not a completed proof, of the utilitarian-legislator problem. The old chapter conclusion and three derivative guides were too confident about later indirect-utilitarian results; the revised notes make the boundary explicit.

The written goal is conceptual sufficiency, not a quotas-based expansion.
- **Optional paragraph-adjacent source material:** approximately **1,653** words, within **eight** closed native disclosures holding **16 separately cited records**, selected by their additional source value.
- **Companions:** **22** exact prose anchors, locally sufficing without new branches of theory. Their text is omitted from print; essential reasoning is not.
- **Revision:** all six original components remain intact and all five question categories are independently populated.

A substantial portion of Source Depth is attributable to primary-source case details, original citations, historical reports and extra explanation, **not** to new independent claims. Explanatory analogies and study aids are distinguishable from source quotations; no new box taxonomy, design tokens or custom JS controller is introduced.
