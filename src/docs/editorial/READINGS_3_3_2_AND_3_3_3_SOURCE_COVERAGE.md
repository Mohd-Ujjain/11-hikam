## 3.3.2 — source-to-dossier map

- **Questions 5–8:** the author's grounds for moral obligation, what he counts as religion's contributions, the comparison of Islam with humanism, and the distinction between public benefit and the intended end of an action.
- **Questions 9–11:** divine self-sufficiency and the purpose of worship for the worshipper; impossible descriptions and the meaning of power; the formulation of Euthyphro's dilemma and the author's response.
- **Questions 12–16:** measure of recompense, annihilation versus continued punishment, the author's distinction among cases of inherited belief, choosing and divine foreknowledge, and capacity versus its created effects.
- **Questions 17–20:** empirical inquiry, trust with action, reasons the author gives for disagreement among intelligent persons, and why physical size is not the measure of moral worth.
- **Questions 21–23:** the author's legal and social arguments, including his distinctions between individual belief, public advocacy, and institutional authority; his stated positions on women's rights and obligations. These remain attributed positions in the assigned reading, not current legal advice or the dossier's own rules.

## 3.3.3 — source-to-dossier map

- **Questions 5 and 6:** the unity question, creating without need or loneliness, worship as a means rather than the final end, and how the author reasons from the world to divine agency.
- **Questions 9 and 10:** kinds of perceptibility; the author’s arguments against visual perception of God in this life or the next; the distinction between appearing in bodily form and being known by effects.
- **Questions 11–13:** occupation of a place, the difference between what is observed in spatial cases and a universal rule for existence, and the two distinct directions of indwelling that the author rejects.
