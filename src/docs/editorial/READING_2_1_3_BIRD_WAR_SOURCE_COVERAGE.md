## Corrected gaps in the pre-existing dossier

1. **Source title and edition**: the old cover said “Cambridge Companion” and omitted Colin Bird. The supplied reading belongs to Bird's *An Introduction to Political Philosophy*, chapter 11 in the 2019 edition.
2. **War as cultivated enmity**: the original dossier explained professional order but did not teach Walzer's moral coercion, justice's responsibility boundaries or the psychological tactic of refusing aggression.
3. **All seven criteria with important qualifications**: reinstated reasonable prospect of success, strictly defensive just cause and the distinct applications of the two proportionality tests, while retaining the private-authority example in optional Source Depth.
4. **Bird's criticism of equivocation**: now distinguishes his serious demand for guidance from a false insistence on total theoretical unanimity. The 2003 Iraq disagreement and Elshtain's defence are visible.
5. **Independence plus exceptional qualification**: keeps the Nazi example, antiutilitarian stance, sliding scale and Walzer's **qualified** rather than categorical 1942 judgment.
6. **Competing costs of just cause**: neither statism nor cosmopolitanism is automatically vindicated. Moellendorf's rebuttal about necessary conditions is preserved alongside Bird's independent claim that **acknowledged** just cause breeds enmity even without fighting.
7. **Two distinct pacifisms**: neither King's active political pressure nor Penn's legally channelled force is equated with simply doing nothing.
8. **The largest prior omission**: the original draft's conclusion collapsed pp. 23–30 into a brief collateral-harm note. The new §§VIII–IX teach innocence, Hobbes/Walzer's self-defence argument, Moellendorf's two stipulated thought experiments, Joad's reply, practical collateral harm, imminence/alternatives, excuse versus justification, and the paradox of civilian command.
9. **Ending remains open**: Bird turns toward a realist argument. He does **not** announce that ordinary morality has definitively failed or prescribe bombing civilian officials.
10. **Revision independence**: the Key Table is now a stand-alone comparison; all six existing revision elements and all five examination categories remain independently useful.
