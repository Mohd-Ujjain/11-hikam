Three recall guides per reading shape the study aids, not claims. The assigned extracts determine the arguments, examples, terminology and scope. Arabic wording follows the project's language guidance. No private inputs are committed. Both predecessor dossiers were archived byte for byte.

## 3.2.7 — شبهات حول أدلّة وجود الله تعالى

[أجوبة الشبهات العقدية، الجزء الثالث، الدرس الثاني](https://books.almaaref.org/view.php?id=2414). The [book's copyright leaf](https://books.almaaref.org/static/documents/uploads/pdf/shobohat-3%281%29.pdf) credits مركز المعارف للمناهج والمتون التعليمية with preparing it; دار المعارف الإسلامية الثقافية issued the edition. These are distinct roles, and quoted figures are not credited as authors of the dossier. Assigned extract: seven leaves, printed pp. 27 and 29–34; p. 28 is not supplied.

- **27, 29–30:** the three objectives, Qur'an 41:53, and four moves in the reply to the first objection: not finding versus negating, plurality of proofs and burden of criticism, provisional concession, and application of the objector's rule to denial. The last is a dialectical move, not the lesson's own rule of inference.
- **30–31:** the horizons of the design objection; chance versus an intelligent organizer; comparison with human order as support, not a stand-alone proof; the writing example; the dilemma over a supposedly intelligent nature and the further denial that matter is aware; the independence of observed order from human benefit.
- **32–34:** the origination syllogism; the distinction between temporal beginning and essential dependence, including the example of angels and the disagreement over temporal origination; motion and change; creation without a prior thing as distinct from treating nothing as a cause. Review and the reading from al-Kafi are covered without treating its building example as a claim of directly sensing the Maker.

## 3.2.8 — Introduction, The Case for God

[Karen Armstrong, The Case for God](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/4888/the-case-for-god-by-karen-armstrong/9780307389800/). The assignment is the complete Introduction in the supplied ten-leaf PDF, not either of the book's two parts. The PDF shows no printed page numbers. Locators here and in the Companion schema are assigned PDF leaf positions; they are not borrowed from another edition.

- **Leaves 1–2:** facile God-talk, the limits of ordinary predicates and the idea of a Supreme Being, public uses of God, historical variation, theologians' restraint, symbolic liturgy, and mystery plays.
- **Leaves 3–4:** complementary and differently tested mythos and logos, grief and ultimate meaning, heroic narratives, Freud and Jung, and ritual as enactment. Armstrong's account is not a rejection of science or a plea to accept myth as factual history.
- **Leaves 4–6:** knowing by doing, disciplined practice and ekstasis, Zhuangzi's wheelwright and cicada catcher, names for the transcendent, the bodily and rational structure of music, Beethoven and the limits of speech. Steiner's conclusion and Armstrong's assessment of how a skeptic may receive it remain distinguishable.
- **Leaves 7–8:** early modern epistemic changes, belief as assent, religious literalism, creation science, historical relativity of the charge of atheism, classical and new atheism, and Armstrong's acknowledgment of valid objections alongside her criticism of the new atheists' scope.
- **Leaves 8–10:** compassion, what can be retained from modern God-symbolism, the author's change of view, the two-part plan and Christian focus, non-universality of reticence, revival and intolerance, unlearning and the suggestion about early underground temples.
