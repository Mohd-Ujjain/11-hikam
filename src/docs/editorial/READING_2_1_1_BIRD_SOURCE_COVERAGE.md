## Source-fidelity changes relative to the previous edition

- Corrected `Cambridge Companion` and the generic TOC source credit to the verified original author and book. The chapter's actual heading is **“Introduction.”**
- Preserved Bird's degrees of confidence: violent coercion **may** be justified in some circumstances; different historical cases have different reputations; rejection of proposed justifications **may** warrant institutional change.
- Avoided treating the discussion of historical fascist intellectuals as approval or as a new counterargument.
- Removed the previous assumption that “authority differs from power” is already answered in this excerpt. Bird explicitly asks **whether** it differs, and why legal, neighbourly and expert claims are puzzling.
- Preserved the distinction between political work and philosophical reflection while rejecting a simplistic theory/practice opposition.
- Marked the famous political-interest line as **Bird adapting Trotsky**, not a verbatim quotation originated in those words by Trotsky.
- Preserved the distinction between familiarity with state authority and a demonstrated proof of its legitimacy; state justification is examined, not automatically endorsed.
- Replaced the previous revision “see main table” pointer with a complete independent Key Table.
